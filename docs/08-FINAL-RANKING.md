**Date:** 31 July 2026
**Status:** Supersedes `00-PRODUCT-DECISION.md` (round 1). Awaiting founder sign-off.

## The founder's goal (the yardstick)

Build once, to perfection, with ongoing work limited to bug fixes and customer-requested
features; customers must be easy to retain; the product must firmly generate **≥£50k/year**
(~28–35 retained accounts at ~£125–£180/month blended).

## Why the ranking changed from round 1

Round 1 recommended construction retention-recovery. Round 2's fine-detail research
killed it as a standalone and demoted the whole vertical:

1. **The Commercial Payments Bill (Lords, May 2026) phases in an outright ban on
   construction retentions** — a pure retention product has a ~3–5-year terminal life.
2. Retention recovery is **one-and-done** (backlog cleared → cancel), scoring 2/10 on
   the founder's now-heaviest criterion; even reframed around monthly payment
   applications, construction's worst-in-UK insolvency rate caps retention at 5/10.
3. Cold email into construction is a grind in practice (5/10): weak database coverage,
   generic inboxes, site-based owners, and years of R&D-tax spam conditioning.

## Why #1 wins on THE GOAL, despite a 1-point margin

The top two are honestly close. The tiebreak is the founder's goal, and it cuts one way:

- **£50k firmly, fastest:** recruitment buyers are the easiest cold-email audience in
  UK B2B (8/10 — all Ltd, REC/Agency Central lists, LinkedIn-native, email-living
  personas). The quoting co-pilot needs phone-assisted trust-building with shop-floor
  MDs — a slower, more founder-labour-intensive path to 30 accounts.
- **Hands-off after clients landed:** the Compliance OS is deterministic workflow
  software (reminders, registers, evidence files) — no AI-accuracy trust barrier, fully
  self-serve-able. The quoting co-pilot invites permanent "the estimate was wrong"
  calibration work — exactly the ongoing labour the founder wants to avoid.
- **Regulatory forcing function, live now:** umbrella joint-and-several PAYE liability
  has been in force since 6 April 2026; agencies are in their first enforcement year and
  must maintain due-diligence evidence permanently. Compliance spend is mandated, not
  discretionary — that firms up both acquisition and retention.
- **Structural lock-in:** weekly pay-cycle adjacency is the strongest switching-cost
  pattern in vertical SaaS (InTime running since 2009; ripping out mid-placement risks
  paying temps late).

**Honest counterweights (kept on the record):** recruitment agencies are failing at
their fastest rate since 2008 (uncontrollable ~2–4%/yr customer-death churn — priced
into the 7.5 retention score); SafeRec could expand from umbrella auditing into agency
ops; CRM vendors will market "JSL-ready" hard through 2027 — the window is now to
mid-2027. If the founder would rather own the *stickiest product* and accept slower,
phone-assisted acquisition, option 2 is one point behind and Birmingham is its best
market on earth.

## The £50k model for #1 (pricing skill applied)

- **Value metric:** temps-on-books (tiered flat) — value scales with workers managed,
  not recruiter seats. Good-better-best: **£99/mo** (≤50 temps) / **£179/mo** (≤150
  temps, recommended anchor) / **£299/mo** (unlimited + priority support). Annual
  prepay at ~17% off to harden retention.
- **Price positioning:** between the next-best alternative (three point tools + spreadsheets
  ≈ £100–250/mo combined, none giving a unified JSL evidence file) and perceived value
  (joint liability for umbrella PAYE = five-to-six-figure exposure per failure).
- **Path to £50k:** ~28 accounts at £150/mo blended = £50.4k. That is 2.4% of
  Birmingham's ~1,148 agencies, or under 1% of REC's 3,300+ members once expanded
  UK-wide — with expansion revenue as agencies' temp books grow.
