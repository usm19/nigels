## The winner: Subcontractor Commercial Engine (working title "Moiety")

For UK construction specialist subcontractors (5–80 staff, West Midlands first):
monthly applications for payment, applied-vs-certified deltas, Construction Act
notice-deadline engine, retention ledger with moiety release chasing, payer insolvency
radar, and full auto-export ("your data always with you").

**Why it satisfies "doesn't exist":** round-2 adversarial teardown (doc 07) verified:
"an automated retention ledger + chasing engine + payer insolvency monitoring for
subbies — the combination exists nowhere as SaaS" — the highest verified whitespace
(7/10) of all 15+ verticals researched. Nearest rivals: BuildQS (early-stage, no public
pricing/reviews), Site Samurai (broad/shallow £99), Payapps (main-contractor-led,
resented by subbies — their reviews say so).

**Moat layers (the "months to copy" case):**
1. Rules engine: deadline/notified-sum computation across JCT, NEC, and bespoke
   subcontract forms under HGCRA + the Commercial Payments Bill's 30/60-day caps and
   8%-above-base interest — months of domain encoding, invisible from the UI.
2. Cross-customer payer-risk radar: every customer's main contractors enrich one shared
   payment-behaviour + Companies House signal graph. Copycats start at zero; the
   incumbent dataset compounds daily. (This is the network-effect mechanic.)
3. System-of-record switching costs: applications, certificates, retentions, chase
   history accumulate; leaving means losing the money trail (auto-exports preserve the
   customer's DATA, but not the live deadline engine and radar).
4. GTM camouflage: private repo, in-person demos, no public feature marketing to clone.

**Known weaknesses (kept honest, from rounds 2–4):**
- Construction has the UK's worst customer insolvency rate (~52.6/10,000/yr) —
  uncontrollable churn floor; mitigated by volume pricing and the radar itself.
- Retentions phase out under the Commercial Payments Bill over ~3+ years; the
  payment-application + deadline core is strengthened by the same Act, so the product
  outlives its retention hook. Build the retention module as one module, not the core.
- Rules-engine build in 8 weeks: achievable scoped to JCT/NEC standard forms + a
  manual-confirm path for bespoke contracts; do not attempt universal contract AI.
- 8-week "near-zero touch" is post-stabilisation; expect onboarding involvement with
  the first ~10 customers (consistent with the founder's in-person model).
