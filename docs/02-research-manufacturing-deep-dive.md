# UK SME Manufacturing Deep Dive: Precision Engineering Job Shops (Birmingham/Black Country)

> Research stream 2 of 4. Compiled July 2026 from cited public sources; figures are as
> reported by those sources and should be re-verified before use in external materials.

## Market context

West Midlands manufacturing employs ~270,000 people (~10% of regional employment, 14% of output); metals is the second-largest sub-sector at 19.9% of industrial output, behind automotive/transport at 28.7% ([Express & Star](https://www.expressandstar.com/your-world/2025/07/24/west-midlands-defies-manufacturing-decline-achieves-10-growth-in-last-decade/), [Insider Media/Make UK-BDO](https://www.insidermedia.com/news/midlands/confidence-robust-in-west-midlands-manufacturing-sector)). The sector is fragile and automotive-exposed: the Sept 2025 JLR cyberattack (£1.9bn total cost) halted Solihull/Wolverhampton plants and hit ~5,000 supply-chain firms; 77% of 84 surveyed Black Country/Birmingham Chamber businesses reported negative impact, 35% cut staff hours, 14% started redundancies ([Express & Star survey](https://www.expressandstar.com/news/business/2025/09/26/jlr-cyber-attack-leaves-black-country-businesses-counting-the-cost-survey-shows/), [Procurement Magazine](https://procurementmag.com/news/jlr-cyber-breach-financial-disaster)). Lesson: these firms actively want customer diversification — i.e., winning more quotes from new buyers.

## 1. Day-to-day operational pains (ranked)

**Quoting/estimating — most acute and most revenue-tied.** Buyers send each RFQ to 3–5 shops and the first credible quote often wins; responding within 48h lifts win rates ~25% ([Tangle Research](https://www.tangle.io/resources/research/quoting-time-reduction-metal-fab)). A Paperless Parts survey of 400+ part buyers found 67% expect a quote in under 24 hours and only 6% will wait more than 3 days ([Production Machining](https://www.productionmachining.com/blog/post/sometimes-no-quoting-rfqs-is-essential-for-efficiency)) — yet Practical Machinist owner threads show real turnaround of 2–5 days, with owners explicitly trying to compress "5 days down to 1–2" ([PM: methods to expedite quoting](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/methods-to-expedite-quoting.375813/), [PM: average quote turnaround](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/average-quote-turn-around-time-for-custom-tooling.363339/)). CNCCookbook's shop survey benchmarks: top shops win ~70% of quotes, average shops ~51% ([CNCCookbook](https://www.cnccookbook.com/job-quote-cost-estimation-survey-results/)). Quoting is tribal: typically the owner/MD prices everything from experience ([PM: should a machinist quote for the owner](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/should-a-machinist-do-quoting-for-the-owner-of-company.411234/)), and ~70% of shop operational knowledge is undocumented ([HINDSITE](https://www.hindsiteind.com/blog/what-is-tribal-knowledge-in-manufacturing-and-what-happens-when-it-walks-out-the-door/)) while ~20% of the UK engineering workforce retires by 2026 ([Aspion](https://www.aspion.co.uk/blog/the-uk-manufacturing-skills-shortage-what-the-data-says-what-you-can-do-207)). MTDCNC coverage confirms quoting is seen as the operational bottleneck in UK subcontract shops ([MTDCNC](https://mtdcnc.com/event/tomskubala/investment-and-automation-driving-change-across-uk-manufacturing/)).

**Skills shortage — biggest strategic worry, hardest for software to fix.** 92% of manufacturing SMEs report skills shortages; 75% call it their biggest growth barrier; 61,000 vacancies unfilled late 2024 ([Aspion/Skills Horizon](https://www.aspion.co.uk/blog/the-uk-manufacturing-skills-shortage-what-the-data-says-what-you-can-do-207), [GOV.UK SME Skills Horizon 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sme-skills-horizon-2025)). Its software-relevant consequence: nobody spare to do admin, quoting, or ERP data entry.

**Late payments — chronic cash-flow drag.** 90% of UK firms hit by late payment in 2025, average delay 32 days ([Coface 2025 UK Payment Survey](https://www.coface.com/news-economy-and-insights/2025-uk-payment-survey-companies-face-rising-payment-delays-amid-buyer-cash-flow-concerns)); manufacturing is among the slowest-paying sectors at 80+ days ([Apollo Finance](https://www.apollofinance.biz/late-payments-uk-which-sectors-pay-invoices-slowest-in-2025/)); average small firm owed ~£22k at any time ([QuickBooks UK](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/uk/blog/small-business-late-payments-report-2025/)). Painful, but well-served (invoice finance, credit control tools).

**Material price volatility.** US Section 232 moves pushed hot-rolled coil up 20%+ since March 2025; UK safeguard/CBAM changes are increasing quote-invalidating volatility for the 1,200+ downstream UK fabricators ([PAD Magazine](https://www.padmagazine.co.uk/construction/will-britains-new-steel-policy-make-reinforcement-more-expensive/33923/), [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/united-states-modifies-steel-aluminum-and-copper-section-232-tariffs), [Steelnews](https://steelnews.biz/british-steel-tariffs-cbam-rising-prices/)). This makes stale spreadsheet rates in quotes actively dangerous — a quoting-tool feature, not a standalone product.

**Quality documentation.** ISO 9001/PPAP/FAIR/material certs are still run from paper folders in many small shops; audit prep "consumes weeks" ([1factory](https://www.1factory.com/industries/precision-machining-quality.html), [PM: small shops ISO 9001 cost](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/any-small-machine-shops-iso-9001-steps-cost.191318/)). Real pain, but a compliance purchase — slower sales cycle than revenue tools.

## 2. Existing software landscape and gaps (5–50 person UK shop)

- **Paperless Parts** (US): opaque, "highest-tier" pricing; PM users say "not worth the huge cost" and that it targets shops with ≥1 FTE estimator ([PM thread](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/what-is-the-deal-with-paperless-parts.431259/), [G2 pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/paperless-parts/pricing)). US-centric; no meaningful UK GTM.
- **ProShop** (~$500–715/mo + separate implementation, [Top10ERP](https://www.top10erp.org/products/proshop-erp/pricing)) and **Fulcrum Pro** (from ~$800/mo, revenue-based, [SoftwareConnect](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/fulcrum-pro/)): powerful but heavy for 10 people.
- **JobBOSS2/ECI**: users call it "clunky, hard for new employees", with scheduling done in Excel anyway ([PM ERP thread](https://www.practicalmachinist.com/forum/threads/erp-jobboss-2-vs-proshop-vs-something-actually-good.411796/)).
- **UK incumbents**: **Statii** (Chesterfield; founded by two subcontract fabricators) from ~£100–130/mo all-in, 30-day contract, unlimited phone support, loved for simplicity ([Statii](https://www.statii.co.uk/pricing), [SelectHub](https://www.selecthub.com/p/mrp-systems/statii/)); **123insight** from £200/mo, no contract, live in weeks ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1021085/123insight)). These prove the price point and the "simple + phone support" playbook — but neither has geometry-aware or AI quoting.
- **Katana** ($299 base ballooning to $747–1,095/mo; users report 523% price increases; D2C-product-oriented, poor job-shop fit — [Brahmin Solutions](https://www.brahmin-solutions.com/blog/katana-pricing)); **MRPeasy** ($49/user/mo) — its UK precision-engineering case study (EGL Vaughan) ran on Excel before 2023 and cut admin ~70% after ([MRPeasy](https://www.mrpeasy.com/blog/success-story-egl-vaughan/)) — evidence the base state is still spreadsheets + Sage + paper travellers.
- **Instant-quote platforms**: **Fractory** (Manchester) and **Geomiq** (London, 1,100+ vetted partners) have trained buyers to expect instant quotes ([ADS Advance](https://www.adsadvance.co.uk/fractory-launches-instant-quoting-for-cnc-machining.html), [Geomiq](https://geomiq.com/)). They squeeze job-shop margins as intermediaries — a reason shops want their *own* fast-quoting capability.
- **CloudNC Quote Agent** (London): the most direct AI-quoting competitor — RFQ-to-quote in under 10 minutes, free tier (20 parts), tiered paid plans ([CloudNC](https://www.cloudnc.com/quote-agent)). Machining-focused (CAM Assist heritage), CAD-file-driven; weaker on fabrication, PDF-only RFQs, and quote-to-works-order-to-certs flow.

## 3. Quoting: the evidence base for a product

Faster quoting demonstrably wins work (67% of buyers expect <24h; 48h response = ~25% win-rate lift; 3–5 competing quotes per RFQ — sources above). Shops also silently no-quote a large share of RFQs when the estimator is busy ([Production Machining](https://www.productionmachining.com/blog/post/sometimes-no-quoting-rfqs-is-essential-for-efficiency)) — unquoted RFQs are pure lost revenue. An AI-assisted tool needs: PDF drawing + STEP ingestion, feature/tolerance extraction, cycle-time and setup estimation calibrated to *that shop's* history (capturing the owner's tribal pricing), live material pricing (steel/aluminium volatility), and a professional branded quote out the door with win/loss tracking. Nobody serves UK 5–50-person shops at Statii-like prices with this: Paperless Parts is too dear, CloudNC Quote Agent is machining-only and quote-only (no works order/certs), Statii/123insight have no drawing intelligence.

## 4. Buyability

Demonstrated budget: £100–£500/month (Statii ~£130, 123insight £200, MRPeasy ~£40/user). Crucially, these owner-managed firms pay **£155+/month for Qimtek supplier membership just to receive RFQs** ([Qimtek](https://www.qimtek.co.uk/supplier-membership)) — they buy revenue, not efficiency. The owner/MD is the estimator, buyer, and decision-maker in one person; sales motion is phone/visit/trade-show (MACH, Subcon) more than cold email. List-building is easy: [Made in the Midlands](https://madeinthemidlands.com/) (450+ member profiles), Qimtek's 7,000-supplier directory ([Qimtek directory](https://www.qimtek.co.uk/uk-manufacturing-services/directory)), and free Companies House bulk data filtered by SIC 25620 (machining), 25500 (forging), 25110/25730 with West Midlands postcodes ([opengovuk SIC 25620](https://opengovuk.com/company?sic=25620)).

---

**(a) Single most acute, revenue-tied problem:** Quote turnaround. Buyers expect quotes in <24–48h and award to the fastest credible responder; the average shop wins only ~51% of what it quotes and silently no-quotes more when the owner — the only person who can price work — is on the shop floor. Every slow or skipped quote is directly lost revenue, and the post-JLR-attack scramble for customer diversification makes winning new-customer RFQs existential in the Black Country right now.

**(b) 1–2 month solo wedge:** An AI quoting co-pilot for UK subcontract machinists/fabricators, priced ~£99–199/month flat (Statii's proven price band, no per-seat): forward an RFQ email or drop a PDF drawing/STEP file; the tool extracts part details, suggests routing/cycle times seeded from a short owner interview plus each won/lost quote (progressively bottling the owner's tribal knowledge), pulls current steel/aluminium prices, and produces a branded PDF quote plus a follow-up nudge — quote out in 15 minutes instead of 3 days. Deliberately *not* an ERP: it sits in front of Sage/spreadsheets/Statii, exports won quotes as a works-order sheet. Sell it door-to-door via a Made in the Midlands / Qimtek / SIC-code Birmingham list with the pitch "answer every RFQ the same day, even the ones you currently bin."

**(c) Main risks/objections:** (1) CloudNC Quote Agent has a free tier and UK brand — must differentiate on fabrication/PDF-only RFQs, owner-knowledge capture, and quote-to-order workflow rather than pure cycle-time AI. (2) Trust: owners fear mis-quoting (one bad fixed-price quote can erase a month's margin) and drawing confidentiality (aerospace/defence NDAs) — the tool must be assistive with human sign-off, and UK-hosted. (3) Sales friction: owner-managed shops don't answer cold email; GTM is phone/visits/trade press, slow for a solo founder. (4) Cycle-time estimation accuracy across arbitrary parts is genuinely hard — scope v1 to 2D/prismatic work and shop-calibrated templates, not universal CAM simulation. (5) Statii or 123insight could bolt on AI quoting; speed and Birmingham-local focus are the only durable moats.
