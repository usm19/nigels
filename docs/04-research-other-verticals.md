# Vertical Evaluation: B2B SaaS for Birmingham/West Midlands SMEs (cold-email GTM, solo founder, 1–2 month build)

> Research stream 4 of 4. Compiled July 2026 from cited public sources; figures are as
> reported by those sources and should be re-verified before use in external materials.

## A. Property/Lettings & HMO Landlords — Score: 4/10

**Density.** Genuinely large and local: Birmingham's mandatory scheme covers ~4,000 larger HMOs, and the additional licensing scheme (live 5 Jun 2023–4 Jun 2028, all 69 wards) was scoped at ~12,000 smaller 3–4 person HMOs ([Birmingham City Council](https://www.birmingham.gov.uk/news/article/1139/birmingham_city_council_launches_consultation_on_a_proposed_additional_licensing_scheme_for_houses_in_multiple_occupation_hmos_across_the_city)); selective licensing runs in a further 25 wards ([Licence Checker](https://www.licencecheckerengland.co.uk/councils/birmingham)).

**Acute pain.** Real and time-stamped: the Renters' Rights Act (implementation from 1 May 2026) adds information-sheet duties with civil penalties up to £7,000 per tenancy ([RealYield](https://www.realyield.co.uk/insights/hmo-post-rra-what-landlords-need-to-know-2026)); Birmingham issues no automatic renewal reminders, so licence/CP12/EICR/EPC expiry tracking sits entirely on the landlord, with £30k civil penalties and Rent Repayment Orders for lapses ([CertWatch](https://certwatch.co.uk/guides/hmo-licence/birmingham)); the council has issued £450k in fines and run 12,000+ inspections ([PropReady](https://propready.co.uk/landlord-compliance/birmingham/hmo-licensing)).

**Saturation.** The killer. COHO owns the HMO niche at £30/mo for 12 units ([LetSorted comparison](https://letsorted.co.uk/guides/best-landlord-property-management-software-uk-2026)), and the RRA wave has already spawned a swarm of micro-SaaS (LetSorted, Lendlord, CertWatch, PropReady, LetSafe, Sucasa) doing exactly the "compliance tracker" wedge. Price is anchored brutally low.

**Cold-email fit.** Poor. Landlords are semi-consumers: the public licence register gives names and postal addresses, not business emails; this niche is won in HMO Facebook groups and property meetups, not outbound inboxes.

## B. Haulage/Logistics SMEs — Score: 5/10

**Density.** Excellent: Birmingham sits at the heart of the "golden logistics triangle" with ~150m sq ft of Midlands warehouse space ([Birmingham Dispatch](https://www.birminghamdispatch.co.uk/birmingham-is-at-the-centre-of-the/), [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/articles/theriseoftheukwarehouseandthegoldenlogisticstriangle/2022-04-11)); ~49,000 haulage firms in England, ~90% micro ([Haulage Exchange](https://haulageexchange.co.uk/blog/how-many-haulage-companies-uk/)). The O-licence register is public — the best cold-outreach list of any vertical here.

**Acute pain.** Compliance terror is real: Traffic Commissioners benchmark operators against DVSA Earned Recognition's 4% tacho-infringement KPI at Public Inquiry, with curtailment/revocation powers ([Orbis](https://orbisio.co.uk/blog/tachograph-compliance-uk), [External Transport Manager](https://www.externaltransportmanager.co.uk/traffic-commissioner-public-inquiry/)). But the *dominant* pain is survival: ~400 hauliers failed in 2025 after 470 in 2024, 39% at serious insolvency risk, 1–2% margins, 30% of HGV km run empty ([Motor Transport](https://motortransport.co.uk/industry-news/pouring-fuel-on-the-fire-of-haulage-insolvencies/89816.article), [Opus](https://opusllp.com/report/road-haulage-sector-report-market-is-shrinking/), [DfT 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/road-freight-statistics-2025/domestic-road-freight-statistics-united-kingdom-2025)).

**Saturation.** The 5–20 truck operator is less underserved than folklore says: TruTac, Aquarius, FleetCheck and Truckfile sell per-vehicle tacho/maintenance compliance cheaply and are well-regarded ([TransportForum](https://transportforum.com/viewtopic.php?t=10527)), and Mandata Go explicitly targets 10–50 vehicle fleets ([Mandata](https://www.mandata.co.uk/transport-management-systems/go/)). The genuinely underserved sub-5-truck tier is exactly the cohort going bust — mandated-spend willingness exists, but ability to pay is fragile and the culture is phone-first.

## C. Independent Care Providers — Score: 3/10

**Density.** Strong: 192 home-care agencies in Birmingham alone ([Lottie](https://lottie.org/location-guides/best-home-care-in-birmingham/)); domiciliary services grew 11% nationally last year ([Skills for Care 2025](https://www.skillsforcare.org.uk/Adult-Social-Care-Workforce-Data/workforceintelligence/resources/Reports/National/The-state-of-the-adult-social-care-sector-and-workforce-in-England-2025.pdf)).

**Acute pain.** CQC's Single Assessment Framework (34 quality statements, continuous evidence-led assessment) is widely described as complex, frustrating and "failed to deliver" ([Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/2025/cqc-single-assessment-framework-what-this-means-for-health-and-social-care-services-providers-and-the-post-implementation-review/), [CQC/CPA review](https://www.cqc.org.uk/news/our-response-care-provider-alliances-review-single-assessment-framework)); 24.7% turnover, 10.1% domiciliary vacancy rate compound the rostering/records burden.

**Saturation + buying power.** Worst combination of the four: Birdie (from ~£200/mo), CarePlanner (£199/mo), CareLineLive, Nourish, Access, everyLIFE, Person Centred Software, and Log my Care with a *free tier* under 5 staff ([homecareOS guide](https://www.homecareos.co.uk/blog/home-care-software-uk-guide.html), [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/199285/Log-my-Care/)) — plus post-2022 AI-native entrants undercutting further. Buyers are council-fee-squeezed (£12.00 median carer pay, 56p above NLW — [King's Fund](https://www.kingsfund.org.uk/insight-and-analysis/long-reads/social-care-360-workforce-carers)), and registered managers' inboxes are already carpet-bombed by care-software vendors. Avoid.

## D. Recruitment Agencies — Score: 6/10 (winner)

**Density.** The strongest local concentration: 1,148 actively trading recruitment firms in Birmingham ([Firmbase](https://firmbase.co/resources/industry-lists/recruitment-companies-in-the-uk-birmingham)) — mostly exactly your 5–50 headcount target.

**Acute pain.** A genuine, dated regulatory shock: from 6 April 2026 agencies carry PAYE/NIC liability for workers paid via umbrella companies (JSL), forcing documented umbrella due-diligence, payslip auditing and audit trails ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.co.uk/insights/umbrella-company-reforms-2026-what-businesses-need-to-know/), [REC](https://www.rec.uk.com/our-view/news/news-our-business-partners/umbrella-company-compliance-what-joint-and-several-liability-means-recruitment-agencies-and-how-prepare), [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366614704/Autumn-Budget-Employment-agencies-to-take-on-PAYE-processing-from-umbrella-firms-from-April-2026)). Perennial temp-desk pains (timesheet chasing, right-to-work/IR35 evidence) persist because Firefish gates timesheets/temp workflow behind Scale/Enterprise tiers ([esremedia](https://esremedia.co.uk/blog/bullhorn-alternatives-uk-recruiters)).

**Saturation.** Core ATS/CRM is fully saturated (Bullhorn five-figures/yr, Vincere for 5–50 seat agencies, Firefish for 1–10, JobAdder, Voyager — [ATSpro](https://atspro.co.uk/best-recruitment-software-uk)), and the obvious JSL wedge already has a leader: SafeRec claims 7,000+ agencies/MSPs ([SafeRec](https://saferec.co.uk/umbrella-due-diligence)). But narrow workflow gaps remain around small temp desks (timesheet + registration-pack + RTW-expiry chasing as one automated layer on top of whatever ATS they have).

**Cold-email fit.** Best of all four by far: recruiters live in email, buy per-desk SaaS on short cycles, and respect competent outbound — your GTM channel *is* their native habitat.

## Wildcards

**Food wholesale order-entry (Birmingham Wholesale Market + Asian/Afro-Caribbean wholesalers) — Score: 6/10.** Birmingham hosts the UK's largest integrated wholesale market (18 acres) plus a dense independent wholesale cluster — Rekki alone lists 249 Asian suppliers in Birmingham ([Birmingham Wholesale Market](https://birminghamwholesalemarket.company/), [Rekki](https://rekki.com/food-wholesalers/gb/birmingham/asian)). The pain is revenue-tied and daily: orders arrive by WhatsApp/voicemail and get rekeyed into invoicing systems, and mis-keyed orders mean credit notes and wasted deliveries — the exact error problem Choco was built on ([Total Food](https://totalfood.com/choco-infuses-restaurant-vendor-ordering-with-innovative-technology/)). Buyer-side apps (Rekki) and Choco's wobbly UK presence leave supplier-side "WhatsApp/voice → order lines in your existing system" AI genuinely thin in the UK. Caveats: relationship/phone culture means cold email alone may need to be paired with market walk-ins — which, being local, is actually your unfair advantage.

**NEC event/exhibition contractors — Score: 4/10.** The NEC's 500 events/2.4m visitors a year ([Expo Hire](https://www.expohire.com/venues/national-exhibition-centre)) support a dense local cluster of stand builders, AV/furniture hire and crew firms; pains include penalty-enforced vehicle-slot booking via Voyage Control, crew scheduling and quote/kit chaos. But rental/AV firms are decently served by Current RMS and Rentman, the buyer pool is smaller than the other verticals, and demand is lumpy/seasonal — interesting, not compelling for a 1–2 month build.

## Final Ranking

1. **D. Recruitment agencies — 6/10.** Largest local buyer pool (1,148 firms) that natively buys from cold email, with a fresh April-2026 regulatory wedge — but you must pick a narrow temp-desk/compliance-chasing niche, not another ATS, and route around SafeRec.
2. **Wildcard: Food wholesale order-entry — 6/10.** Acute, revenue-tied daily pain, weak supplier-side UK competition, and a physical Birmingham cluster you can walk; scores even with D on opportunity but riskier on channel (phone culture dilutes pure cold email).
3. **B. Haulage/logistics — 5/10.** Superb density and a public outreach register, but cheap entrenched compliance incumbents (TruTac/FleetCheck) and buyers in financial free-fall (400 insolvencies in 2025) cap upside.
4. **A. HMO landlords — 4/10.** Loud, well-evidenced RRA/licensing pain but a COHO-dominated, £30/mo-anchored, micro-SaaS-swarmed niche whose buyers can't be reached by cold email.
5. **Wildcard: NEC event contractors — 4/10.** Real niche pains but small TAM, seasonal demand, and adequate rental-software incumbents.
6. **C. Independent care providers — 3/10.** Deep pain but the most saturated software market of the six, free-tier price destruction, and the weakest ability to pay.
