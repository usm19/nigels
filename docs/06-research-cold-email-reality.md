# Round 2 Research — Cold-Email Channel Reality (UK B2B, Birmingham SME focus)

> Research stream 6 (round 2, stream 2 of 3). Compiled July 2026 from cited public
> sources; figures are as reported by those sources and should be re-verified before use
> in external materials.

## 1. Legal fine print (PECR + UK GDPR)

**The corporate-subscriber carve-out is confirmed and load-bearing.** PECR Reg 22's consent requirement applies only to "individual subscribers." Corporate bodies — Ltd companies, LLPs, Scottish partnerships — are "corporate subscribers" and can be cold-emailed without prior consent ([ICO B2B marketing guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [salespeople.co.uk PECR explainer](https://www.salespeople.co.uk/explained/cold-email-pecr-regulation-22)). **Sole traders and ordinary (non-Scottish) partnerships are treated as individuals — consent needed**, same as consumers ([hybridlegal.co.uk](https://www.hybridlegal.co.uk/blog/using-business-emails-for-b2b-cold-outreach-in-the-uk-what-you-can-and-cant-do)). This is the decisive constraint for construction, where sole traders are endemic (see §2).

Even for Ltd targets: UK GDPR still applies the moment you email `firstname@company.co.uk` (personal data), so compliant practice = a documented legitimate-interest assessment, sender identification, a working unsubscribe in every message, and a privacy notice ([ea.partners](https://ea.partners/post/is-cold-email-legal-in-the-uk); [mailgraf guide](https://mailgraf.com/blog/uk-gdpr-email-marketing-guide)).

**Enforcement pattern:** ICO fines target mass consumer spam, not targeted B2B — e.g. Allay Claims £120k (4M texts) and ZMLUK £105k (67M consentless emails from bought third-party data) ([ICO, Jan 2026](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/01/fines-of-225-000-for-nuisance-marketing-messages/); [Lewis Silkin 2024 roundup](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2024/05/30/the-ico-fines-again-a-round-up-of-fines-issued-by-the-ico-in-2024)). No published enforcement against low-volume, targeted corporate-subscriber outreach was found. However, the **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 raised the max PECR fine to £17.5m / 4% of turnover from 5 Feb 2026** ([ConsentTrail](https://consenttrail.co.uk/blog/ico-pecr-fines-2025-analysis)) — the risk asymmetry now punishes sloppy bought-list blasting hard.

## 2. List quality per vertical

**Companies House gives you zero emails — verified.** The registered email address companies must now file (ECCTA, since 4 March 2024) is explicitly **not publicly available** ([Rapid Formations](https://www.rapidformations.co.uk/blog/registered-email-address-for-uk-companies/); [Michelmores](https://www.michelmores.com/corporate-insight/eccta-a-companys-registered-office-address-and-the-requirement-to-register-an-email-address/)). Companies House is a *filtering* asset (legal form, SIC code, accounts = staff-size proxy), not a contact source.

- **Construction subcontractors:** ~885k construction businesses — the UK's largest sector by count — but dominated by self-employed one-man bands (~745k self-employed workers) ([money.co.uk stats](https://www.money.co.uk/business/business-statistics); [Bytestart](https://www.bytestart.co.uk/news-insights/how-many-small-businesses-are-there-in-the-uk/)). Crucially, ~three-quarters of all UK businesses are non-employers, and sole proprietorships overwhelmingly have no staff ([BPE 2025, gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2025/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2025-statistical-release)). **A subbie with 5-80 payroll staff is near-certainly Ltd** (incorporation is tax-rational at that size), so the PECR trap is avoidable by filtering to companies with filed accounts showing employees. The real problem is emails: trades firms surface `info@`/`office@` generics (lawful for corporate subscribers, per ICO), and US-centric databases (Apollo) skew to office-based professionals with weak trades coverage; Cognism is the stronger EMEA option but costs $15-25k/yr ([Cognism vs Apollo comparisons](https://www.cleanlist.ai/blog/2026-05-23-cognism-vs-apollo)).
- **Manufacturing job shops:** Best list infrastructure of the four. [Qimtek](https://www.qimtek.co.uk/uk-manufacturing-engineering-directory) lists 1,500+ subcontract manufacturers searchable by process/location/approvals (free buyer registration); [Made in the Midlands](https://madeinthemidlands.com/) hosts 450+ member microsites with contact pages (mostly phone + forms rather than raw emails — expect a scrape-then-verify step). Job shops live on emailed RFQs, so a monitored quoting inbox almost always exists.
- **Recruitment agencies:** Easiest lists in B2B. [REC member directory](https://www.rec.uk.com/jobseekers/member-directory) covers 3,300+ agencies by sector/location; [Agency Central](https://www.agencycentral.co.uk/) adds thousands more; consultants are LinkedIn-native so Apollo/Cognism coverage is excellent.
- **Food wholesalers:** Thinnest lists. FWD has only ~20 members (representing ~600 wholesalers) ([fwd.co.uk](https://www.fwd.co.uk/); [Wholesale Managers profile](https://wholesalemanagers.com/company/federation-of-wholesale-distributors-fwd/)); no strong public directory; a Birmingham-area TAM may be only a few hundred firms — too small to sustain a volume email motion.

## 3. Response reality

2025-26 baselines: ~27.7% open / ~3.4% average reply; 5-10% is good, meetings booked at 0.5-2.5% of sends ([Martal](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/); [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)). UK-specific: Sopro's 126M-email dataset puts average response ~5.1%, most campaigns 1-5% ([Sopro State of Prospecting](https://sopro.io/resources/whitepapers/the-state-of-prospecting-26/)). Reply rates have roughly halved since 2019 ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/)). Notably, **"SaaS/software" pitches are the worst-performing category (<2% replies)** while services with concrete monetary outcomes (legal ~10%) top the tables ([Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/cold-email-benchmarks-by-industry)) — a warning against leading with "try our software."

Per vertical: manufacturing outreach that's tightly targeted runs 5-10%, and a real case study (Cobblestone Millworks, building-products manufacturer) generated $168k in opportunities from 44.9k emails at 2.3% reply / 13.8% positive-reply on the best sequence ([LeadGenJay](https://results.leadgenjay.com/article/cobblestone-millworks); [Sera benchmarks](https://blog.seraleads.com/kb/personalized-outreach-strategies/b2b-cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks/)). One-contact-per-company outreach nearly doubles replies (7.8% vs 3.8%) ([Martal](https://martal.ca/b2b-cold-email-statistics-lb/)). Recruitment agencies *live* in email, so attention exists, but their inboxes are the most saturated — client-acquisition replies run 3-10% with heavy send-volume caps advised ([Puzzle Inbox](https://puzzleinbox.com/blog/cold-email-for-recruiting-agencies/); [Modern Inbound](https://moderninbound.com/blog/cold-email-for-recruitment-agencies)). Construction owner-MDs are site-based and their directors have been **bombarded for years by R&D-tax and lead-gen cold outreach** ([MPA on cold-call volume](https://www.mpa.co.uk/news-insights/knowledge-hub/why-youre-getting-so-many-cold-calls-from-rd-tax-companies/)) — expect generic-inbox triage by an office manager and better results from phone-plus-email hybrids. No credible construction-specific reply benchmark was found; treat that absence itself as signal.

## 4. The "we found money you're owed" pitch effect

No clean A/B dataset exists, but the indirect evidence is strong and two-sided. **The UK R&D tax credit boutique industry is proof the pitch acquires SME clients at scale**: unregulated advisers built entire books via sophisticated cold-call/cold-email campaigns telling SMEs they were owed HMRC money, at 25-30% contingency fees — so effective HMRC said the model "needs disrupting" ([Research Professional News](https://www.researchprofessionalnews.com/rr-news-uk-innovation-2022-2-fraud-the-strange-world-of-r-d-tax-cold-calls/); [MSC R&D](https://mscrnd.com/hmrc-clamps-down-on-rd-tax-credit-sales-and-marketing/); [Parliament written evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/113172/html/)). The catch: that boom **poisoned the well** — HMRC now warns SMEs about these tactics ([Business News Wales](https://businessnewswales.com/hmrc-provides-warning-of-clever-marketing-tactics-used-by-unregulated-rd-tax-credit-firms/)), and "you're owed a refund" is a dominant scam template ([Weatherbys scam alert](https://www.weatherbys.bank/help-and-support/latest-scams/scam-alert-cost-of-living-and-energy/)), so vague found-money claims now trigger skepticism. The pitch still outperforms "try our software" *when the sum is specific and verifiable* — and construction hands you exactly that: ~£780m sits in unreleased retentions at any time, with subcontractors losing ~£79,900 on average ([PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/building-control-news/uk-construction-governments-late-payment-retention-reform-plans/160617/); [LazyQS](https://www.lazyqs.co.uk/blog/construction-retention-explained)). "Your retention on [named project] may be recoverable" is a fundamentally stronger email than any feature pitch.

## Cold-email-channel ranking (viability /10)

1. **Recruitment agencies — 8/10.** Perfect legal position (all Ltd), best-in-class list coverage (REC directory + Apollo/Cognism), personas who genuinely live in email; the only tax is a heavily pitched, savvy inbox.
2. **Manufacturing job shops — 7/10.** Ltd-dominated, Qimtek/Made-in-the-Midlands give ready-made targeted lists, RFQ-driven email culture means messages get read; expect modest 2-5% replies and add phone follow-up to reach MDs.
3. **Construction subcontractors — 5/10.** The found-money pitch fit is the best of the four (retentions/late payment), and the 5-80-staff filter neutralises the sole-trader PECR trap, but weak database coverage, generic inboxes, site-based owners, and years of R&D-tax spam make pure email a grind — viable only as email-plus-phone.
4. **Food wholesalers — 4/10.** Local population too small for an email motion, no usable directory, generic sales inboxes and a phone/rep-driven ops culture; the list you can build runs out before the channel can compound.
