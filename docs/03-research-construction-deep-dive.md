# UK Construction Subcontractors (5–80 staff): Market Research Report

> Research stream 3 of 4. Compiled July 2026 from cited public sources; figures are as
> reported by those sources and should be re-verified before use in external materials.

## 1. Day-to-day operational pains (2024-2026 evidence)

**Retentions — the standout revenue-tied pain.** £3.2bn–£5.9bn is withheld in retentions across the sector annually ([BEIS/Pye Tait impact assessment](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a82b647e5274a2e8ab58f4b/Retentions_Consultation_Impact_Assessment_FINAL.pdf); [Access Group](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/construction/resources/retention-in-construction/)), typically 3–5% of contract value, half released at practical completion and half 12–24 months later after defects rectification. Pye Tait/BEIS found **44% of contractors have lost retention monies to upstream insolvency**, with ~£220–240m lost each year — roughly **£1m per working day** ([ECA retentions campaign](https://www.eca.co.uk/taking-action/about-the-retentions-campaign); [Construction Enquirer](https://www.constructionenquirer.com/2018/04/18/over-100-mps-back-ring-fencing-of-retentions/)). ECA/BESA data behind the Aldous Bill: £7.8bn unpaid over three years, £700m lost outright to insolvencies, and the average contractor carrying ~£27,500 withheld at any time ([Installer Online](https://www.installeronline.co.uk/new-aldous-bill-want-to-help/)). LazyQS claims average subcontractor losses of £79,900 ([LazyQS](https://www.lazyqs.co.uk/blog/construction-retention-explained)). The second-moiety release is the killer: it falls due 12–24 months after the subbie left site, nobody diarises it, and main contractors do not volunteer it — money is only released when chased. Reform is live: from 1 March 2025 large firms must report retention practices under the [Reporting on Payment Practices (Amendment) Regulations 2025](https://www.constructionleadershipcouncil.co.uk/news/the-reporting-on-payment-practices-and-performance-amendment-regulations-2025/), and government is [consulting on ending retentions](https://beale-law.com/article/uk-government-consults-on-ending-retentions/) — abolition remains years away.

**Late payment / cashflow.** Construction is the UK's worst payment sector: 95% of firms report delays, average delay 38.2 days ([Coface 2025 UK Payment Survey](https://www.coface.com/news-economy-and-insights/2025-uk-payment-survey-companies-face-rising-payment-delays-amid-buyer-cash-flow-concerns)); actual payment times run ~61 days against 30-day terms ([InvoiceInsure](https://www.invoiceinsure.co.uk/why-is-the-construction-industry-one-of-the-worst-culprits-for-late-payments/)). Construction has led UK insolvencies four straight years — 4,032 in 2024, 3,931 in 2025, 21.5% above pre-pandemic ([Bricks & Bytes](https://bricks-bytes.com/corporate/uk-construction-insolvency-crisis-2025/)). SMEs spend ~130 hours/year chasing late payment; 34% borrow to cover the gap ([Installer Online](https://www.installeronline.co.uk/new-aldous-bill-want-to-help/)).

**Payment applications & the Construction Act.** Subbies get paid via monthly applications for payment, not invoices. Under HGCRA 1996, if the payer misses its payment notice and pay-less notice deadlines, the application becomes the "notified sum" payable in full — the basis of "smash-and-grab" adjudication ([Placefirst v CAR Construction [2025]](https://www.footanstey.com/our-insights/articles-news/payment-notices-in-construction-contracts-lessons-learned-from-placefirst-v-car-construction/)). Winning this game requires tracking per-contract application dates, due dates, notice deadlines and certified-vs-applied deltas — most small subbies do this badly or not at all. Adjudication referrals hit a record 2,264 in 2023/24, +9% YoY ([KCL/Adjudication Society](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/construction-law/assets/kcl-dpsl-construction-adjudication-report-3.0-2024-update-digital-aw1.pdf)). Under-certification of applications and the variations account are the classic dispute hotbeds — undocumented variations and day-works are routinely struck from valuations; the party with contemporaneous records wins ([Costigan King](https://www.costiganking.com/insights-2/payment-delays-defects-legal-issues-in-construction-projects)).

**CIS and compliance admin.** Contractors must verify subbies with HMRC, deduct 20/30%, and file monthly returns; penalties start at £100 for one day late ([Xero CIS guide](https://www.xero.com/uk/guides/construction-industry-scheme/)). Painful but largely solved by Xero/accountants. **RAMS/PQQ:** duplicated prequalification costs the industry ~£1bn/year; 180,000 specialists produce 2m+ documents annually; an average PQQ costs a subbie ~£650 ([Constructionline CAS](https://www.constructionline.co.uk/about/common-assessment-standard/); [Roofing Today](https://roofingtoday.co.uk/new-pre-qualification-system-introduced-to-save-construction-1bn-a-year/)) — but CAS/CHAS/SafeContractor already monetise this.

## 2. Existing software and the gap

- **Payapps** dominates application-for-payment workflow but is *main-contractor-led*: subbies mostly use it because a MC mandates it, per-contract pricing, and it only covers contracts inside the platform ([Payapps subcontractor pricing](https://www.payapps.com/uk/pricing-subcontractors/)).
- **Causeway, EasyBuild, Archdesk, Access ConQuest** — mid-market ERP, £10k+/yr, overkill below ~80 staff. **Buildxact** — residential builder estimating, not subbies.
- **simPRO (~£30+/user), Joblogic (£39–55/user), ServiceM8 (£25)** — field-service job management for electrical/plumbing/HVAC service-and-maintenance work ([squote comparison](https://squote.app/knowledge/general/job-management-apps)); none handle contract-side applications, retentions, pay-less notices or notified sums.
- **Xero + CIS** handles deductions and returns but applications for payment are not invoices — the commercial layer lives in Excel.
- Reality for 10–40-person subbies: spreadsheets, email threads and one overloaded QS/office manager ([StoneRise](https://www.stonerise.tech/blog/subcontractor-management-software-construction)).

**The unserved slot is subcontractor-side commercial control:** every application across every contract, applied-vs-certified deltas, notice deadlines, variations log, and retention ledger with both release dates. Early movers exist — **BuildQS** ([app.buildqs.com](https://app.buildqs.com/)) does subcontractor payment applications + retention + cashflow; **LazyQS** does AI contract/tender review for subbies; **Site Samurai** targets UK SME subbies (Enterprise £699/mo) — all tiny, thin, and validating the space rather than owning it.

## 3. Money left on the table

Billions sit unreleased at any time; Build UK has estimated ~£780m held at any one moment on some measures, BEIS £3.2–5.9bn annually ([Forge Command](https://forgecommand.co.uk/blog/retention-in-construction-contracts-uk.html)). ISG's September 2024 collapse: £1bn+ deficit, £700m+ owed to the supply chain, £190m+ calculated supply-chain losses, ISG Retail alone holding £7.9m of subcontractor retentions; administrators recovered only ~£38.5m of £885m ([New Civil Engineer](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/latest/failed-isg-companies-had-over-800m-in-liabilities-21-10-2024/); [Construction Wave](https://constructionwave.co.uk/2026/04/24/isg-administrators-recover-fraction-of-885m-owed-to-creditors/)). Carillion left ~£800m of retentions unpaid. **No mainstream tool automatically tracks every retention with moiety release dates and chases them.** Closest: Payapps' retention visibility (only for Payapps-managed contracts), BuildQS, and human recovery services like [Hay's Collections](https://www.hayscollections.com/services/retention-recovery/) and solicitors ([Helix Law](https://helix-law.co.uk/construction-law/retention-recovery/)) — whose existence proves willingness to pay contingency fees to recover this money.

## 4. Buyability

The buyer is the owner-MD or office manager/QS; the trigger is *money owed*, not "software". A cold email saying "you likely have £40–80k of retention out; here's a free scan" is a debt-recovery pitch, not a SaaS pitch — materially better response economics. Budget anchors: £25–55/user/month is normalised (Joblogic/simPRO); a £99–299/month flat fee that recovers five figures is defensible. List-building: **Companies House free data, SIC 41-43 filtered to B/WS/DY/WV postcodes** (hundreds of thousands of construction SMEs nationally; ~99.9% of the sector's 870k firms are SMEs); [Constructionline](https://www.constructionline.co.uk/) (UK's biggest contractor register), CHAS/SafeContractor public directories, NICEIC/Gas Safe registers for M&E trades, FMB/NFB member directories — all scrapable/searchable for trade, size and location.

---

**(a) Most acute revenue-tied problem:** Retention tracking and recovery, as the sharpest edge of the wider "applied-for vs actually-paid" gap. It is pure findable money (avg ~£27.5k–£80k per firm outstanding), time-bombed (second moiety due 12–24 months post-completion, forgotten by default), insolvency-exposed (44% have lost retentions; ISG made this vivid in every subbie's mind since 2024), and completely unserved on the subcontractor side.

**(b) Solo-build wedge (1-2 months):** A "retention ledger and recovery engine" for subcontractors: import contracts (or parse final accounts/application spreadsheets), auto-compute both moiety amounts and release dates from contract terms, dashboard of "£X releasable now / £Y at risk", automated chase letters citing the correct contractual and Construction Act grounds, and Companies House monitoring on every payer with insolvency-risk alerts ("ISG early-warning"). Free retention health-check as the cold-outreach hook; £99–199/mo, or launch with a contingency tier (e.g. 5% of recovered sums) to make it a no-brainer. Expand naturally into full application-for-payment tracking (applied vs certified vs paid, notice deadlines) — the same data, the same buyer.

**(c) Risks/objections:** (1) Fear of relationship damage — subbies hesitate to chase MCs they want repeat work from; mitigate with polite, "our system flags this automatically" framing. (2) Data entry burden — retention terms live in unread PDFs; the product dies if onboarding isn't near-zero (AI contract extraction is essential). (3) Regulatory horizon — government consultation could mandate retention protection or abolition (2027+), capping the long-term wedge, though reporting rules meanwhile create free public data to exploit. (4) Incumbent adjacency — Payapps/Access could bolt this on, and BuildQS/LazyQS are already circling. (5) Low willingness to pay for "admin" software in a sector with 3,900+ insolvencies/year — churn from customer death is real; contingency pricing and recovery-led positioning, not SaaS-led, is the counter.
