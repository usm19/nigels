# Round 2 Research — Churn & Stickiness Assessment

> Research stream 5 (round 2, stream 1 of 3). Compiled July 2026 from cited public
> sources; figures are as reported by those sources and should be re-verified before use
> in external materials. Lens: the founder's requirement that customers be easy to RETAIN
> (low churn, no one-and-done dynamics, minimal ongoing founder labour).

## Benchmark context: what "sticky" means at SMB price points

SMB SaaS churn is brutal at the median. ChartMogul's benchmarks (2,500+ companies) put median customer churn at ~6.1%/month below $25 ARPA, improving to ~2.2%/month above $500 ARPA; SMB-focused SaaS medians run ~3-4%/month logo churn (~40% annualised), and <2%/month puts you in the top quartile ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/blog/churn-basics-and-benchmarks/), [churn benchmarks](https://chartmogul.com/blog/good-customer-churn-rate/)). SaaS Capital's 2025 survey puts median NRR for SMB-serving companies at ~97% vs 118% enterprise, with median GRR drifting down to ~88% ([SaaS Capital](https://www.saas-capital.com/blog-posts/what-is-a-good-retention-rate-for-a-private-saas-company/)); KeyBanc/Sapphire 2024 shows ~90% GRR / ~101% NRR overall medians ([Sapphire](https://sapphireventures.com/press/keybanc-capital-markets-and-sapphire-ventures-private-saas-company-survey-reveals-a-continued-focus-on-operational-efficiency-and-profitability/)). The most relevant dataset — Tidemark's Vertical & SMB SaaS Benchmark — shows vertical SaaS averaging ~91% GRR, rising to ~96% when the platform is fintech/payments-led, and finds retention is structural only when you own a "control point": the system of record for money owed, hours worked, or orders placed ([Tidemark 2025](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/2025-vertical-smb-saas-benchmark-report), [Truths about Vertical SaaS](https://www.tidemarkcap.com/vskp-chapter/vertical-saas-truisms)). Best-in-class SMB retention correlates with: daily/weekly ritual usage, payments/payroll adjacency, accumulating historical data the customer can't export cheaply, and being the thing invoices are generated from. A hard floor: with UK company insolvencies at ~23,900 in 2025 (116 per 10,000 firms) ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/company-insolvencies-november-2025/commentary-company-insolvency-statistics-november-2025), [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/uk-business-insolvencies-2025-new-official-statistics-released/)), 1-2%+ annual churn from customer death alone is unavoidable in any UK SME vertical.

## A. Construction retention-recovery → application-for-payment tool

**Honest verdict on retention-recovery: it is one-and-done, and now also legislatively doomed.** The pain is real — £3.2-5.9bn withheld annually, 44% of contractors have lost retentions to upstream insolvency (avg £79,900), ~£240m/yr lost ([RDS](https://www.retentiondepositscheme.org/insights/the-impact-of-retentions-on-construction-firms), [LazyQS](https://www.lazyqs.co.uk/blog/construction-retention-explained)) — but the workflow is a backlog-clearing project: recover the ledger, then usage collapses to a trickle of new retentions maturing 12 months after practical completion. Worse: the Commercial Payments Bill (introduced to the Lords May 2026, following the March 2026 "Time to pay up" consultation response) imposes an **outright phased ban on construction retentions** — clauses void three years into the transition ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/commercial-payments-bill-would-ban-retention-and-late-payments-uk-construction), [BCLP](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/late-payments-consultation-response-significant-changes-coming-for-the-construction-industry.html)). A pure retention tool has a ~3-5 year terminal life.

**The reframe genuinely fixes cadence but not customer-death churn.** Monthly applications-for-payment, applied-vs-certified tracking, and Construction Act notice deadlines are a real monthly ritual with a system-of-record ledger — this is exactly Payapps' model (retention, variations, notice-deadline reminders, Construction Act compliance; Autodesk acquired it, signalling category durability) ([Payapps](https://www.payapps.com/uk/subcontractors/), [Payapps/Construction Act](https://www.payapps.com/blogs-uk/managing-the-construction-payment-process-applications-for-payment-payment-notices-and-the-construction-act/)). The new Bill's 30/60-day statutory payment caps and 8%-above-base interest actually *increase* the value of notice-compliance tooling. But the customer base is the UK's worst: construction had the most insolvencies of any sector — ~3,973 firms in the year to July 2025, 52.6 per 10,000 (a death rate concentrated in exactly the small specialist subcontractors you'd sell to; specialist contractors were the worst sub-sector) ([PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/hr-skills-news/latest-uk-construction-company-insolvency-stats-revealed/155382), [BCIS](https://www.bcis.co.uk/news/construction-insolvencies-latest-news/)). Expansion is modest (per-project or per-user pricing). Score: sticky workflow, fragile customers.

## B. AI quoting co-pilot for precision engineering job shops

**Strongest organic stickiness profile.** RFQ→quote is a daily, revenue-critical workflow, and the accumulated pricing history (past quotes, win rates, material/cycle-time assumptions) is genuine data lock-in — the tool gets *better* the longer you stay, the classic compounding moat. Comparable evidence: Paperless Parts case studies show multi-year tenures (Focused on Machining a customer since 2019; Leech Industries framing it as "foundation for long-term success") and "power users" building templated estimating logic that would be painful to rebuild elsewhere ([Paperless Parts case studies](https://www.paperlessparts.com/case-studies/paperless-parts-quoting-solution-builds-foundation-for-long-term-success-at-leech-industries/), [power users](https://www.paperlessparts.com/blog/how-paperless-parts-power-users-are-driving-the-job-shop-quoting-software-in-5th-gear/)). UK-native Statii reviews describe it as "integral to our workshop planning" and used "from enquiry to production" — quoting tools that spread into job tracking become de facto ERP ([Reviews.io](https://www.reviews.io/company-reviews/store/www.statii.co.uk), [Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/143292/statii-erp-mrp-system)). One-and-done risk is near zero (quoting never ends); expansion is natural (seats → job tracking → scheduling modules → price rises as win-rate ROI is provable). Customer-death churn is moderate: manufacturing logged ~1,965-1,991 insolvencies in the 12 months to Aug/Oct 2025 (~8% of all cases) ([GOV.UK Sept 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/company-insolvencies-september-2025/commentary-company-insolvency-statistics-september-2025)) — elevated but roughly a third of construction's toll, and West Midlands precision engineering skews toward established, asset-backed firms with decades of trading history. Caveat: churn-prone if it stays a thin "co-pilot" — stickiness requires becoming where quote history *lives*, not a ChatGPT wrapper beside the real system.

## C. Recruitment temp-desk operations layer

**Payroll adjacency = best structural retention, worst customer survival.** Weekly timesheet→invoice→pay cycle is the highest-frequency cadence of all five candidates, and pay-and-bill is the category vertical SaaS theory says to own: money flows through it. Incumbents prove durability — InTime (RSM) has run since 2009; ETZ turns "a timesheet into an invoice in 60 seconds" and embeds into Bullhorn/Xero stacks agencies won't unpick mid-contract ([RSM InTime](https://www.rsmuk.com/what-we-offer/industries/professional-and-business-services/recruitment/intime), [ETZ](https://getetz.com/)). Ripping out a pay-and-bill layer mid-placement risks paying temps late — near-unthinkable, so switching happens rarely and reluctantly. The April 2026 umbrella JSL rules (agencies jointly liable for unpaid PAYE/NIC anywhere in their chain) make compliance tracking a board-level, permanent need ([RSM](https://www.rsmuk.com/insights/advisory/how-new-umbrella-company-legislation-impacts-recruitment-agencies), [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.co.uk/insights/umbrella-company-reforms-2026-what-businesses-need-to-know/)). But the segment is dying at the fastest rate since 2008: 181 recruitment liquidations in six months to Aug 2025 (+18% YoY), driven by NIC rises and a 17% drop in net fee income ([Recruiter](https://www.recruiter.co.uk/news/2025/04/recruitment-insolvencies-rise-fastest-rate-financial-crash), [SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/uk-recruitment-firms-closing-at-fastest-pace-since-financial-crisis)). Agencies are also low-barrier businesses — high formation *and* death. Expect meaningful uncontrollable churn; expansion (temps on books, modules) is good.

## D. Food wholesale order-entry AI

**Daily habit, thin lock-in, shaky customers.** Order capture is used every trading day — Rekki's pitch is literally "liberating not having to key in 1,500 orders a week" and it's embedded with 10,000+ kitchens ([Rekki](https://rekki.com/en-us/faq), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=rekkiapp.com.rekki.release&hl=en_GB)); Choco's OrderAgent/Voice Agent (built with OpenAI) validates exactly this WhatsApp/voicemail→order-lines wedge and now sells churn-risk alerts to distributor sales reps ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/choco/), [Choco](https://choco.com/us/), [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/choco-introduces-new-sales-rep-app-for-food-distributors-302282130.html)). But note Choco's own history: it had to pivot (2023) and reframe value to hold restaurant-side retention — order capture alone is replaceable; retention improved only "mid-to-high single digits" after repositioning. Lock-in is weaker than A-C: order lines flow *through* into the ERP rather than accumulating as the master record, unless you also own the customer/pricing catalogue. Customer death is significant: wholesale/retail logged 3,773 insolvencies in the 12 months to Nov 2025 (16% of cases, +4% YoY), with food wholesalers failing on customer bad debt contagion from hospitality ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/company-insolvencies-november-2025/commentary-company-insolvency-statistics-november-2025), [Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2026/06/23/uk-wholesaler-closes-resulting-in-71-job-losses/)). Also faces well-funded incumbents (Choco/Rekki) rather than a vacuum.

## E. Briefly

**HMO landlord compliance:** genuinely recurring in theory — certificates and licences expire on rolling cycles and Kamma tracks 128 active licensing schemes with 24/7 expiry monitoring for 100+ letting agents ([Kamma](https://www.kammadata.com/how-we-can-help/letting-agents/)) — but for a *direct-to-landlord* SME product the cadence is quarterly-at-best glances at a dashboard, not a workflow; perceived value decays between renewals ("nothing expired this month, why am I paying?"), the buyer (small HMO landlords) is churn-prone and cost-sensitive amid Renters' Rights upheaval, and licences renew every 5 years. Selling to letting agents (portfolio-wide, CRM-integrated, as Kamma does) is sticky; selling to individual landlords is a churn machine.

**Haulage compliance:** better than its reputation — tachograph analysis is a legally mandated *weekly/daily* cycle (TruTac processes 4m+ driver duties monthly; O-licence undertakings force continuous use, and results like Hovis's 96% incident reduction are auditable) ([TruTac](https://www.trutac.co.uk/software/), [UK Haulier](https://www.ukhaulier.co.uk/news/road-transport/technology/hovis-reports-96-reduction-in-compliance-incidents-with-trutac-software/)), so the software itself is near-impossible to cancel while trading. The problem is the customer: ~400 haulier insolvencies in 2025 (470 in 2024), 36.8% of transport firms hold zero cash reserves, and transport/storage has the *lowest* 5-year survival rate of any UK industry ([trans.info](https://trans.info/en/brexit-haulage-insolvencies-467412), [Freight Perspectives](https://www.freightperspectives.com/p/uk-and-ireland-road-freight-in-2026), [ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/explore-local-statistics/indicators/business-survival-five-year)) — plus entrenched incumbents bundled via the RHA.

| 1 | **B. Job-shop quoting co-pilot** | **8.5** | Daily revenue-critical ritual + compounding pricing-history lock-in + module expansion, with only moderate customer-death risk — the Paperless Parts/Statii pattern of multi-year tenures. |

| 4 | **A. Construction payment-application tool** (reframed) | **5** | Monthly cadence and Construction Act deadlines are genuinely sticky, but the worst-in-UK insolvency segment caps GRR; scores 2/10 if left as retention-recovery, which is one-and-done *and* being banned by the Commercial Payments Bill. |
| 5= | **E1. Haulage compliance** | **5** | Legally mandated weekly usage (9/10 workflow) wrecked by the lowest-survival customer segment in Britain and RHA-bundled incumbents. |

**Bottom line:** by the retention criterion, B is the clear pick — it is the only candidate combining daily use, accumulating proprietary data, natural expansion, and a customer base that mostly survives. C is the structural-stickiness winner if you can stomach the sector's customer-death churn. A is only investable as the monthly application-for-payment platform (never as retention-recovery), and even then carries construction's 52.6-per-10,000 insolvency drag.
