# Birmingham & West Midlands SME Landscape 2025–26: Market Map for B2B SaaS Outbound

> Research stream 1 of 4. Compiled July 2026 from cited public sources; figures are as
> reported by those sources and should be re-verified before use in external materials.

## 1. Industry density (company counts, employment, GVA)

Birmingham had **37,245 enterprises in 2024** (up 255 y/y), 99% of them SMEs; across the WM Met area Birmingham grew fastest (+565, +1.5%) while the Met area overall shrank −0.4% ([Birmingham Business Counts Update 2024](https://www.birmingham.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/8449/birmingham_business_count_update_2024.docx)).

- **Manufacturing / precision engineering / metal fabrication** — the region's defining cluster: **£24.2bn GVA, ~270,000 jobs (~10% of regional employment), ~14% of regional output** — well above the national average. Transport equipment (largely automotive) is 28.7% of industrial output, **metals 19.9%, machinery 10.8%** ([Make UK/BDO Regional Manufacturing Outlook via PES Media](https://www.pesmedia.com/uk-manufacturing-jobs-boost-reported-in-new-trade-body-report); [Make UK Facts 2025](https://www.makeuk.org/insights/reports/uk-manufacturing-facts-2025)). Most metal-bashing firms are Black Country SMEs in the 5–100 band.
- **Automotive supply chain** — ~**1,500 businesses serve the automotive sector regionally** ([business.gov.uk West Midlands](https://www.business.gov.uk/invest-in-uk/regions/west-midlands-england/)); JLR's supply chain employs ~120,000, ~60,200 in the Midlands, and the Black Country's automotive supply chain alone employs ~13,000 ([Quantuma](https://www.quantuma.com/jaguar-land-rover-cyberattack-wake-up-call-west-midlands-automotive-supply-chains); [Express & Star](https://www.expressandstar.com/news/business/2025/10/28/next-few-months-critical-for-jlr-manufacturing-supply-chain-in-midlands-says-industry-expert-at-crowe/)).
- **Construction & trades** — construction is the UK's largest SME sector (~870,000 firms) but also lost ~122,000 businesses since 2020 ([RICS](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/construction-journal/the-role-of-smes-in-the-uk-construction-industry.html)); the West Midlands is a CITB priority region for skills gaps ([CITB CSN West Midlands](https://www.citb.co.uk/media/fl4ncutm/westmidlands-lmi-final.pdf)).
- **Logistics / haulage / warehousing** — **~3,984 actively trading logistics firms in Birmingham alone** ([Firmbase](https://firmbase.co/resources/industry-lists/logistics-companies-in-the-uk-birmingham)); in Sandwell transport/logistics is ~9% of employment vs 5% nationally ([Shaw Trust/NCS](https://shawtrust.org.uk/national-careers-service/west-midlands-job-market/)).
- **Professional services** — the WM has the **largest BPFS cluster outside London: 50,000+ firms, 358,200 employed** ([Invest West Midlands](https://www.investwestmidlands.com/set-up-in-west-midlands/why-the-west-midlands/key-sectors/bpfs/)); BPFS is Birmingham's largest GVA sector at £24.3bn (31.7%) ([Birmingham Employment Update 2024](https://www.birmingham.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/30399/birmingham_employment_update_-_2024.pdf)).
- **Recruitment** — Birmingham is a major staffing hub with hundreds of agencies listed publicly ([Agency Central Birmingham](https://www.agencycentral.co.uk/agencysearch/bytown/westmidlands-birmingham.htm)); no reliable regional count found.
- **Property / lettings / HMO** — Birmingham's licensing regime spans **~4,000 mandatory-licence HMOs plus ~12,000 properties in scope of the 2023 citywide additional scheme**, with selective licensing in 25 wards ([Kamma](https://www.kammadata.com/uk-property-guide/property-licensing-guide-for-birmingham/); [Licence Checker](https://www.licencecheckerengland.co.uk/councils/birmingham)); ~338 estate/lettings agents operate in Birmingham ([Homemove](https://homemove.com/agents/list/birmingham/estate-agents/)).
- **Care** — **1,185 CQC-registered care services in Birmingham**, including ~300 home care agencies ([Patient.info directory](https://directory.patient.info/single-location/west-midlands/birmingham); [Lottie](https://lottie.org/location-guides/best-home-care-in-birmingham/)).
- **Hospitality / food wholesale** — accommodation & food services employs ~37,500 in Birmingham (6.7% of jobs) ([Birmingham Employment Update](https://www.birmingham.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/30399/birmingham_employment_update_-_2024.pdf)); Birmingham Wholesale Market is the UK's largest integrated wholesale market — ~73–90 traders, £200m turnover, serving 5,000 independent retailers/caterers ([NCASS](https://www.ncass.org.uk/news/discover-birmingham-wholesale-market-a-smart-choice-for-your-food-business/)).
- **Jewellery Quarter** — **700–800 businesses producing ~40% of UK jewellery**; named a World Craft City in 2025 ([JQ Neighbourhood](https://www.jqneighbourhood.com/about-the-jewellery-quarter); [The Jewellery Quarter](https://www.thejewelleryquarter.org/business/1000-trades/)).

## 2. Digital maturity by sector

UK SMEs broadly lag G7 peers on digital tools, constrained by time/cash/know-how ([SME Digital Adoption Taskforce, 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/sme-digital-adoption-taskforce-final-report/sme-digital-adoption-taskforce-final-report); [Ipsos/DBT SME tech adoption](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688a438aff8c05468cb7b0f0/sme_tech_adoption_dbt_report.pdf)). Sector picture:

- **Small manufacturers/fabricators**: larger firms lead on ERP/automation, but tier-2/3 job shops commonly run quoting, scheduling and works orders on spreadsheets and aged MRP ([ERC digitalisation disparities paper](https://www.enterpriseresearch.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ERC-ResPap111-Investigating-Disparities-in-SMEs-Digitalisation-Mahmood-Asghar-Kousha-Revised.pdf)). **Low-moderate maturity.**
- **Construction trades**: classic paper job sheets, WhatsApp scheduling, Excel quotes ([Elec Training](https://elec.training/news/mobile-apps-for-busy-tradespeople/)) — but heavily targeted by established job-management SaaS (Simpro, Jobber, Joblogic, Payaca). **Low maturity, crowded vendor field.**
- **Haulage**: 62% of logistics firms self-rate as "not great" or "struggling" at digitising ([Neos Networks 2024](https://neosnetworks.com/resources/blog/uk-logistics-digital-infrastructure-report/)); small hauliers still on paper PODs/manual planning, though UK-specific TMS vendors (Mandata, Podfather) exist. **Low maturity, partially served.**
- **Care**: digital care records hit the Government's 80% adoption target by July 2025 ([Digital Health](https://www.digitalhealth.net/2025/01/80-of-providers-on-track-for-digital-social-care-record-by-march-25/); [DHSC 2025 survey](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/technology-use-in-adult-social-care-2025-survey-results)) — Birdie/Log my Care/Nourish now entrenched; remaining gaps are rostering/finance/compliance edges. **Now moderately well-served.**
- **Recruitment & professional services**: saturated with mature vertical SaaS (ATS/CRM, practice management). **High maturity.**
- **Lettings/HMO landlords**: generic property software exists, but multi-scheme licensing compliance (Birmingham's 25-ward selective + citywide additional schemes) is a niche poorly covered; smaller landlords run on spreadsheets. **Low-moderate.**
- **Jewellery Quarter & food wholesale**: micro craft firms and market traders — largely pre-digital, but tiny ticket sizes and thin budgets.

## 3. Email reachability for outbound

- **Manufacturing is famously reachable**: [Made in the Midlands](https://madeinthemidlands.com/members) publishes a browsable member directory (Made in Group ~590–600 manufacturers); Make UK, MACH exhibitor lists and Companies House SIC 25xx/28xx bulk data give near-complete coverage. Companies House free data by SIC code works for every sector here.
- **GBCC** represents a network of **~3,200 businesses** with a searchable member directory ([GBCC](https://www.greaterbirminghamchambers.com/)).
- **HMO landlords/agents**: councils publish public licence registers (name + address of licence holders) — an unusually clean, compliance-triggered list ([Birmingham City Council licensing](https://www.birmingham.gov.uk/info/20175/private_housing/2648/additional_licensing_for_hmos)).
- **Care**: CQC's public register lists every provider with contact details — fully enumerable.
- **Haulage**: O-licence register (DVSA) is public; RHA/Logistics UK directories supplement.
- **Construction trades**: Checkatrade/FMB/NICEIC directories — reachable but inboxes are hammered by lead-gen spam.

## 4. Recent pain-point evidence (2024–26)

- **Taxation & labour costs**: corporate taxation was the top external concern (26%) in GBCC's [Q1 2026 QBR](https://www.greaterbirminghamchambers.com/campaigns-and-insights/quarterly-business-report.html); labour costs (NICs rise) are the biggest price pressure; 27% of firms cut training investment; hiring intentions fell from 33% to <24% of firms 2024→2025 ([GBCC](https://www.greaterbirminghamchambers.com/resource/reskilling-and-upskilling-talent-vital-to-tackling-labour-market-challenges-chamber.html)). Nationally tax concern hit 63% post-Budget ([BCC](https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2025/01/budget-tax-hike-bursts-business-confidence/)).
- **Late payments**: 54% of small firms paid late in the past 3 months; **West Midlands among the worst-affected regions**; construction and professional services hardest hit ([FSB/GoCardless 2025](https://gocardless.com/blog/gocardless-fsb-late-payments-report-2025/)).
- **JLR cyberattack (Sept 2025)**: 77% of 84 firms surveyed by the Black Country, Greater Birmingham and Coventry & Warwickshire Chambers were negatively impacted; £1.5bn government loan guarantee for the supply chain ([Warwickshire Business](https://business.warwickshire.gov.uk/september-2025/news-in-brief/jlr-supply-chain-survey); [JLR](https://www.jlr.com/news/2025/10/jlr-restarts-manufacturing-and-introduces-new-financing-solution-pay-jlr-suppliers)). This exposed cashflow fragility and digital/cyber weakness across hundreds of local tier-2/3 SMEs.
- **Energy**: 52% of firms cite utility costs as a price pressure (Q4 2025); 35% of manufacturers struggle to pay energy bills ([BCC energy survey](https://www.britishchambers.org.uk/news/2026/02/energy-costs-continue-to-spark-business-concern)).
- **Regional monitors**: City-REDI's [WM Economic Impact Monitors](https://blog.bham.ac.uk/cityredi/west-midlands-economic-impact-monitor-10th-december-2025/) and WMCA's [WISE reports](https://cloudcdn.wmca.org.uk/documents/wmca/pdf/wise-annex-october-2025.pdf) track rising WM unemployment (6.2% late 2025) and weak SME confidence.

## Ranked shortlist

1. **Precision engineering / metal fabrication / automotive tier-2/3 suppliers.** Unmatched local density (270k jobs, metals ~20% of industrial output), acute 2025 pain (JLR shock, energy, late payment, quoting/capacity chaos), spreadsheet-level back offices, and the best outbound infrastructure in the UK (Made in the Midlands directory, SIC 25xx lists, GBCC). Budgets exist — these are asset-heavy £1–20m turnover firms.
2. **Logistics / haulage / own-fleet distribution.** ~4,000 Birmingham firms, motorway-hub density, 62% admit poor digitisation, public O-licence register for targeting; pain = margins, POD-to-invoice lag, driver shortage. Watch incumbent TMS vendors (Mandata) at the 20+ truck end; sweet spot is 5–25 vehicles.
3. **HMO landlords & small lettings agents.** ~16,000 licensable Birmingham HMOs plus 338 agents; licensing compliance is mandatory, deadline-driven and register-published (a self-updating prospect list); software coverage of multi-scheme compliance is thin. Smaller budgets per account, but huge volume and a clear compliance trigger.
4. **Construction subcontractors & specialist trades.** Massive density and the worst late-payment/cashflow pain regionally, still on WhatsApp/Excel — but a crowded job-management SaaS market and spam-fatigued inboxes mean you need a sharp wedge (e.g. payment applications/retentions, CIS compliance) rather than generic job management.
5. **(Bubbling under) Domiciliary care.** ~300 Birmingham agencies, brutal staffing/compliance pain, CQC register makes them trivially reachable — but the core record-keeping problem is now 80% digitised and vendor-dominated; only attack an adjacent niche (rostering-to-payroll, local-authority billing).
